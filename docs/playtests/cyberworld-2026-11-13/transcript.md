**Opening**

Rain combs the neon into long, trembling lines. Under an old flood-control viaduct, Lantern Market keeps cooking. Extractor fans cough above noodle pots; battery lamps illuminate vegetables, replacement joints and umbrellas repaired too often to fold. People live above the stalls because the viaduct stays standing, and gather beneath it because somebody here will still take cash.

An old flood line crosses the concrete pillars at shoulder height. Since that flood, stallholders have pooled money to clear drains and raise the central aisle. Tonight that strip of dry footing is kept clear for clinic patients. There are witnesses, light and shelter here; nobody is drawing a weapon.

“Not in the patient lane,” says noodle vendor Mara Venn as porter Ivo Sen noses a medicine handcart under her awning. He shifts it without argument, then produces two damp receipts. “Same credential. Same gate. Six minutes apart.” Mara wipes a clean patch on the counter for them. “Efficient. Usually they take a week to contradict themselves.” The Lantern Market Cooperative runs these loading lanes and shared utilities; its authority looks mostly like people making room for one another.

North, R-17 is stencilled above the service passage toward Meridian Ledger Services' relay. Meridian administers the credentials that open shelter doors and authorize medicine; its printed reconciliation notice promises a dawn update in reassuring blue type. East, the Second Pulse Network's open-door clinic sign glows beneath another awning. The neighborhood medics and records advocates there have started using handwritten dispensing cards when readers refuse patients. Paper has become a medical instrument.

Patch is here beneath the steam, lean and watchful, solder scars marking two fingertips and a physical cyberdeck riding close beneath a patched coat. Once a corporate diagnostic technician, Patch now makes repairs around markets and clinics after refusing to sign away reader discrepancies.

Nearby stands Rook, broad-shouldered in mended street clothes, old implant serials visible beside reinforced wrist tendons. The former security escort lost his contract after disputing an order to abandon an unlisted clinic worker. Switch is here too: rain-flattened hair, antiseptic-bleached jacket cuffs and a wrist display crowded with appointments. A neighborhood fixer and clinic volunteer, Switch knows how much listening can accomplish before anyone touches a terminal.

Then a courier slips beneath the awning, breathing hard. Nessa Vale holds a cracked data shard inside a wooden spacer, keeping its wet contacts off the counter. “The dawn batch lists people as duplicates,” she says. “Living people. Their shelter and medicine permissions go with it. I saw the headings before the connector broke.” Her fingers tighten around the spacer. “I haven't plugged it into anything since.”

R-17 marks the casing—the same code stamped on Patch's Sightline Calibration Lens and above the north passage. A metal seal still clings to one edge: its split-square stamp matches the design beneath Rook's shield slit, though this one's lower corner is sharp, not dent-flattened. Three tiny diagonal cuts mark the wooden spacer, like the signature beneath Switch's Carved Clinic Token. Nessa remains beside the counter, holding the evidence while the medicine cart waits behind her.
